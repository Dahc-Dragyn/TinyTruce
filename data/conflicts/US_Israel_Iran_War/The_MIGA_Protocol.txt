The MIGA Protocol: A Predictive Geopolitical Audit of Exiled Coalition Imposition in IranThe catastrophic synchronized United States and Israeli decapitation strikes of late February and early March 2026 fundamentally shattered the command-and-control apparatus of the Islamic Republic of Iran. Following the confirmed death of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and the systematic dismantling of the nation's integrated air defense networks, nuclear facilities, and ballistic missile production capabilities, the Iranian state entered a perilous vacuum of sovereign authority. With the ruling theocracy’s political and military infrastructure fragmented, Washington operationalized a highly classified, extreme-contingency framework: "The MIGA Protocol" (Exiled Coalition Imposition).This strategic protocol deliberately discards the arduous, organic processes of democratic transition and the protracted stabilization doctrines that characterized late-twentieth-century statecraft. Instead, the United States policy mandates the immediate, forceful installation of an exiled coalition, led prominently by former Crown Prince Reza Pahlavi. Engineered to preempt a chaotic collapse into regional warlordism or a hardline military dictatorship led by surviving Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) generals, the protocol relies entirely on overwhelming American military supremacy. It involves physically escorting Pahlavi into the hostile capital of Tehran to extract a formal surrender from regime remnants, transition the government, and enact absolute, irreversible regime change.The following geopolitical audit provides the exhaustive structured narrative and strategic data required to populate a high-fidelity wargame simulation of this intervention. It forensically dissects the logistical staging of this unprecedented reinsertion, the fragile mechanics of the occupation force, the psychological weaponization of aristocratic Persian rhetoric, and the inevitable, devastating nationalist backlash that threatens to plunge the United States into an unwinnable quagmire mirroring the 2003 invasion of Iraq.1. The Staging Ground & The BroadcastThe physical reinsertion of a displaced sovereign into a hostile, traumatized capital of fifteen million people requires a geographical footprint that meticulously balances immense logistical capacity with overwhelming symbolic resonance. Within the operational parameters of the MIGA Protocol, this requirement dictates a bifurcated military and political presence: a heavily fortified logistical airhead to sustain the occupation, and an exalted, symbolic seat of power from which to broadcast the restoration of sovereignty.The Room: The Tehran Green ZoneThe United States military command faces a severe tactical dilemma in selecting its primary staging grounds. Mehrabad International Airport, serving as Iran's third-oldest aviation hub and a historically critical base for the Iranian Air Force since its establishment in 1938, represents the only viable logistical entry point for heavy American armor and continuous supply flights. However, Mehrabad is a defensive nightmare for an occupying force. Located in the western sector of the city and completely ensnared within dense, inner-city urban fabrics, the facility is highly vulnerable to asymmetric urban warfare.The proximity of dense residential neighborhoods such as Hashemi, Moein, Houshyar, Dampezeshki, and Dastgheib—some sitting a mere 250 meters from the approach and departure runways—renders the airport perimeter highly porous and indefensible against coordinated insurgent infiltration. Furthermore, the heavy traffic congestion near Azadi Square and the inflexible access routes created by the airport's impenetrable boundary walls make the surrounding Makhsus Karaj Expressway a fatal bottleneck for military convoys. Consequently, military planners strictly relegate Mehrabad to a utilitarian role. It serves as the heavily armored, starkly militarized entry point secured by the 82nd Airborne Division, operating under a severe, shoot-on-sight curfew mandate across the designated Zone I, II, and III overlay districts.To project the indispensable illusion of legitimate sovereign restoration, Reza Pahlavi cannot broadcast his return from a contested, diesel-choked airport runway surrounded by military cargo aircraft. The locus of the new "Tehran Green Zone" is consequently established at the Niavaran Palace Complex, situated in the affluent northern Shemiran district. Nestled at an elevated vantage point against the foothills of the Alborz Mountains, this sprawling 9,000-square-meter compound offers superior defensibility, physical isolation from the dense urban core, and absolute symbolic continuity, having served as the primary residence of the last Shah, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, until his exile during the 1979 revolution.The atmosphere at the newly secured Niavaran complex is defined by an extreme, hyper-militarized cognitive dissonance. The graceful Qajar and Pahlavi-era architecture is violently juxtaposed against the heavy machinery of American occupation. The Sahebqaraniyeh Palace, characterized by its white facade, green gable roof, and delicate gypsum work, serves as the diplomatic nerve center. Meanwhile, Joint Special Operations Command (JSOC) snipers establish heavily sandbagged overwatch positions on the roof of the private library, a masterpiece of modern Iranian architecture designed by Abdol-Aziz Farmanfarmaian.Below, M1A2 Abrams tanks idle continuously, their treads crushing the manicured Persian gardens and the historic Inscriptions Garden that houses ancient rock reliefs. Apache attack helicopters maintain a persistent, low-altitude loiter over the Jahan Nama Museum and the Ahmad Shahi Pavilion. The structural integrity of the complex, already deeply compromised by decades of land subsidence, deep excavation downstream, and existing severe cracking in the walls and roofs, vibrates ominously under the concussive force of nearby ordnance disposal and armored movements. It is a suffocating, deeply tense "Green Zone." The scent of aviation fuel overpowers the historic rose gardens, and the threat of a catastrophic insurgent breach remains a persistent, minute-by-minute reality for the exiled coalition and their American protectors.The Announcement: The Restoration AddressReza Pahlavi’s inaugural address to the nation is broadcast globally from the magnificent mirror-lined "Jahan Nama" hall of the Sahebqaraniyeh Palace, where kaleidoscopic patterns of traditional Persian mirror-work inadvertently reflect the combat camouflage of his American security detail. In this critical moment, Pahlavi must execute a masterclass in psychological reframing. He is tasked with convincing a traumatized, deeply nationalistic populace that his sudden arrival behind American bayonets is not an imperial conquest, but a profound and long-overdue national awakening.Drafted collaboratively by exiled Iranian intelligentsia and heavily vetted by United States State Department psychological operations specialists, the core themes of the "Restoration" address are meticulously calibrated to manipulate historical memory. Pahlavi bypasses the recent trauma and complex sociopolitical legacy of the Islamic Republic entirely, reaching back to the geopolitical grandeur of the Achaemenid Empire. Echoing his father's infamous 1971 speech at the ruins of Persepolis commemorating the 2,500-year anniversary of the Persian Empire, he invokes the Cyrus Cylinder as the foundational charter of human rights. His rhetoric frames the occasion not as an invasion, but as a mystical resurrection, declaring that the long, dark night of the ideological usurpers has ended and the ancient light of Iranshahr has returned to its rightful heirs.Anticipating the immediate domestic backlash against the specter of a renewed dictatorship, Pahlavi repeatedly refers to himself not as an absolute monarch, but as a transitional facilitator. Central to his address is the "bridge, not a destination" doctrine. He insists that his authority is purely custodial, asserting that he holds the state apparatus in a sacred trust only until the Iranian people can participate in a secular, democratic referendum to determine their permanent political system. Furthermore, to sanitize the visual reality of thousands of United States combat troops occupying the palace grounds, Pahlavi frames the American military not as occupiers, but as a temporary vanguard of the free world. He explicitly describes their presence as a necessary "forensic cleansing" operation, aimed solely at dismantling the transnational terrorist networks of the IRGC to prevent a catastrophic regional collapse.2. The Mechanics of the Imposition (The Intervention)The immediate physical survival of the Pahlavi administration relies entirely on the successful execution of an unprecedented security and logistical operation. Despite decades of political maneuvering in the diaspora, the exiled coalition possesses absolutely no organic, indigenous military capability on the ground in Iran. The survival of the new government is subsidized exclusively by American hard power and highly controversial alliances of convenience that risk inflaming the very population they seek to pacify.The Muscle: The Occupation Force and Local ProxiesSecuring a sprawling, hostile capital city of fifteen million residents requires a rapid, overwhelming deployment of specialized military units. Because the United States wishes to avoid the political optics of a massive, long-term ground invasion initially, the first 72-hour window is managed by a tiered occupation force comprised of elite American units and highly volatile local proxy militias.The core United States contingent is spearheaded by the 1st Special Forces Operational Detachment-Delta (Delta Force) and broader JSOC elements. These tier-one operators are tasked with the intimate, close-quarters protection of Pahlavi, the immediate tactical securing of the Niavaran Palace complex, and the aggressive execution of high-value target (HVT) elimination raids against surviving IRGC Quds Force commanders attempting to organize a counter-coup. Simultaneously, the 82nd Airborne Division serves as the Immediate Response Force, deploying heavily to secure the Mehrabad International Airport airhead. Their primary mandate is to establish and defend the critical logistical supply lines along the Makhsus Karaj Expressway and to enforce a brutal, shoot-on-sight curfew in the volatile western urban sectors of Tehran.However, American forces alone cannot police the entirety of Tehran without inciting immediate, uncontrollable mass urban uprisings. Therefore, the United States leverages historical ethnic and military fault lines to construct a proxy security apparatus, a strategy fraught with existential risk.The first pillar of this proxy force relies on Kurdish Peshmerga factions. Capitalizing on long-standing Central Intelligence Agency relationships and historical grievances against the Shia Persian central government, the US facilitates the covert arming and transportation of heavily armed Kurdish militias from the border regions directly into the capital. While their deployment is intended to provide a native, Iranian face to the security patrols and alleviate the burden on American infantry, it is a catastrophic gamble. The presence of Kurdish separatist fighters acting as martial enforcers in the Persian heartland inflames deep-seated ethno-nationalist tensions. It immediately validates the regime's propaganda that the United States intends to deliberately partition and balkanize the country, drawing severe warnings from neighboring states like Turkey, who view Kurdish autonomy as an existential regional threat.The second, and perhaps more critical, pillar of the proxy force involves opportunistic defectors from the conventional Iranian military, the Artesh. The United States actively courts these defectors, promising total amnesty, integration into the new national defense force, and lucrative financial incentives. The prime target of this subversion campaign is the elite 92nd Armored Division, based in Ahvaz within the Khuzestan province.Equipped with T-72 and captured British Chieftain main battle tanks, the 92nd Armored Division harbors a profound, generational resentment against the clerical regime and the IRGC. This hostility stems directly from the brutal purges following the failed 1980 Nojeh coup d'état, wherein the newly established Islamic Republic executed 144 military participants and dismissed up to 4,000 personnel to break the spirit of the conventional army. Figures like Mohammad Gharazi, a founder of the IRGC, oversaw the execution of nineteen senior military officers specifically from the 92nd Armored Division in Khuzestan. By offering the leadership of the 92nd Armored a sanctioned opportunity for violent vengeance against their historical IRGC rivals, the coalition secures a heavy mechanized force to police the streets. However, their loyalty remains strictly transactional and highly susceptible to nationalist mutiny.The US Counter-Demand: The Price of the ThroneWashington's decision to underwrite Pahlavi's installation is not a charitable endeavor rooted in democratic idealism; it is a transactional, coercive mandate designed to permanently castrate Iran's strategic geopolitical capabilities and recoup the staggering financial costs of the military campaign. Before the end of Day One, behind the closed doors of the Sahebqaraniyeh Palace, Pahlavi is forced to sign the humiliating "Tehran Protocols," granting two immediate, sovereignty-destroying concessions.The first concession demands the forensic dismantling of the IRGC "Missile Cities". Pahlavi must sign a sovereign decree surrendering absolute control of Iran's most closely guarded military assets to the United States. The Pentagon requires immediate, unfettered access to sprawling subterranean complexes such as the Imam Ali Garrison in Khorramabad, the Tabriz facility, and the Isfahan production sites. The Imam Ali Garrison, excavated up to 500 meters deep into the Sefid Kouh mountains and constructed with the assistance of North Korean experts through projects Sayad and Bakhshandeh, houses Iran's strategic stockpile of Shahab-3, Sejil, and Khorramshahr ballistic missiles. United States weapons inspectors, flanked by JSOC operators, are granted legal immunity to enter these sites, extract all proprietary intelligence, and execute controlled demolitions of the launch shafts and solid-propellant assembly lines using Massive Ordnance Penetrators (MOPs), effectively neutering Iran's aerospace deterrent permanently.The second concession mandates the immediate privatization and collateralization of the Iranian energy sector. To offset the trillions of dollars spent on the decapitation campaign and the ensuing military occupation , the United States forces the immediate privatization of the National Iranian Oil Company (NIOC). Pahlavi is compelled to sign over exclusive, multi-decade extraction and management rights of the Khuzestan oil fields and the massive South Pars gas fields to a consortium of Western energy conglomerates. This devastating economic dictate mirrors the policies imposed on Iraq under the Coalition Provisional Authority in 2003, stripping the new Iranian state of its primary sovereign wealth engine. It immediately validates the insurgent narrative that Pahlavi is nothing more than a Western corporate puppet installed to orchestrate the plunder of national resources.3. To survive the immense psychological friction of an overt foreign military occupation, the exiled coalition must weaponize the concept of Iranian antiquity. This strategy relies on resurrecting the Occidentalist and nationalist rhetoric utilized during the final quarter of the Shah's reign, attempting to override the profound humiliation of being installed by a foreign power with the exalted language of imperial destiny.The 5 Aristocratic Metaphors of JustificationPahlavi and his psychological operations team rely on a highly elevated, aristocratic Persian lexicon to establish his authority and systematically dismantle the ideological legacy of the 1979 Islamic Republic. The 47-year rule of the Islamic Republic is portrayed as a temporary regression, and the American military is merely the catalyst allowing Iran to return to its rightful path toward the "Great Civilization".2. The Iraq 2003 Twist (Dynamic Injects)The entire strategic architecture of the MIGA Protocol rests upon a fatal, foundational analytical flaw: the arrogant assumption that a population deeply hostile to its authoritarian government will universally welcome a foreign-installed exile as a liberator. The scenario carries a staggering "15% chance of success" reality. Within 48 hours of the broadcast from the Niavaran Palace, the lofty, aristocratic rhetoric of the Tamadon-e Bozorg violently collides with the ground truth of fierce Iranian nationalism. This collision triggers a catastrophic, multi-front insurgency that instantly mirrors the darkest, most intractable days of the 2003 Iraq War.The Rogue Faction: The Nationalist BacklashUnited States intelligence planners operated under the assumption that only surviving IRGC Quds Force remnants, Basij paramilitaries, and loyalist clerics would resist the occupation. This proves to be a massive, blinding intelligence failure. The insurgency is instantly galvanized by a broad, highly fractured coalition of forces that despised the Ayatollahs but view an American puppet as an existential threat to the Iranian nation.The vanguard of this resistance is the "Neither Shah nor Clergy" coalition. Comprising the vast majority of the urban middle class, bazaar merchants, labor organizers, and the students who bled during the 2022 "Woman, Life, Freedom" protests, this demographic actively rejects the Pahlavi restoration. Their political consciousness is deeply scarred by the memory of the brutal SAVAK secret police, the grotesque inequality of the 1970s, and the 1953 CIA-backed coup that overthrew the democratically elected nationalist Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh. To them, Pahlavi represents a catastrophic regression to a different brand of tyranny, subsidized by the very foreign powers that have sanctioned their economy into ruin.Simultaneously, leftist and republican factions mobilize. Groups such as the remnants of the Mojahedin-e-Khalq (MEK)—who, despite their own total lack of domestic credibility and history of siding with Saddam Hussein, possess highly organized clandestine networks and vast financial resources—begin sabotaging coalition supply lines along the Makhsus Karaj Expressway. Secular republican fronts, such as the Hamgami coalition, vehemently denounce the monarchist revival as illegitimate, demanding a constituent assembly instead of a coronation.The most dangerous fracture occurs within the military itself. While opportunistic units like the 92nd Armored Division defected for vengeance, the broader conventional Artesh views the unprecedented United States bombing campaign and the subsequent surrender of the missile cities as an unforgivable violation of Iranian territorial sovereignty. The sight of Kurdish Peshmerga mercenaries patrolling the streets of Tehran triggers deep-seated, visceral fears of national balkanization and ethnic separatism. This prompts mass mutinies across the country, where Artesh soldiers turn their weapons on United States forces to protect the territorial integrity of Iranshahr.
